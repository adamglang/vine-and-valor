### The wall that reads like milled timber

A board-formed retaining wall is a poured concrete wall where the forms themselves are rough-sawn boards. When the forms come off, the concrete carries the wood grain knots, saw marks, and all. It's a finish that feels like it belongs on a mountain property: structural, weather-honest, and quietly expensive-looking.

###### Where it fits

- **Lakefront retaining walls** at Priest Lake and Coeur d'Alene.
- **Terraced landscape walls** on hillside custom homes around Sandpoint.
- **Entry walls, seat walls, and planter walls** at the front of a property.
- **Foundation walls that are exposed to view**, where a smooth form finish would look like a parking garage.

###### How we build one

1. **Select the boards.** Rough-sawn cedar or pine, graded by texture. We pre-treat the forms with a release agent that preserves the grain.
2. **Form the wall.** Boards are set tight, braced hard, and aligned perfectly a board-formed wall shows every imperfection in the forming.
3. **Tie the reinforcement.** Full rebar cage to the engineer's spec. A pretty wall that fails structurally is a failure.
4. **Pour carefully.** Slower lifts, thorough vibration to pull the grain onto the face without blowing out the form.
5. **Strip and cure.** Release the boards gently, cure the wall, and apply a penetrating sealer that deepens the color and protects against freeze-thaw.

###### What it costs vs. a plain wall

More. You're paying for the boards (often once, sometimes only once depending on reuse), slower pour, and finish-grade craftsmanship. What you're not paying for is veneer the finish is the structure. No stone to pop off, no stucco to crack, no paint to repaint.

###### Worth it for the right project

If the wall is going to live in front of the house, in the entry courtyard, or as the landscape architecture of a lakefront property yes. For a behind-the-shop retaining wall, a broom finish and a good sealer will serve you fine.
