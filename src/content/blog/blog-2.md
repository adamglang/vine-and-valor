### The question we get every spring

"Should we do stamped concrete or pavers?" Usually asked standing in the grass where a patio is about to go in. Both are good options. But they age very differently up here.

###### Stamped concrete one monolithic slab

Stamped concrete is a single poured slab, tooled while it's still plastic to read like slate, ashlar, random stone, or wood plank. Color goes in the mix, on the surface as a release, and sometimes as an acid stain on top. Sealed, the whole patio moves as one piece.

**What it does well in North Idaho:**

- No joints for weeds, ants, or moss to take over.
- Freeze-thaw has nowhere to wedge in and lift individual pieces.
- Finish options are essentially unlimited we can match the stone on the chimney or the siding on the cabin.
- Lower maintenance: reseal every few years and you're done.

**Where it asks for respect:**

- Control joints need to be cut where they want to be, not where they look clean.
- A repair is never truly invisible you're working on a monolithic slab.

###### Pavers modular, forgiving, slower

Pavers are individually placed on a compacted base. A single cracked paver can be lifted and replaced. The look is modular you can see the grid no matter how well it's laid.

**What they do well:**

- Individual replacement is genuinely easy.
- Heaves and settlements are correctable without a demo hammer.

**Where they get expensive over time:**

- Joint sand washes out. Weeds come up. You'll be topping them off.
- Freeze-thaw can lift individual pavers out of alignment, especially on lakefront soils.
- A large paver patio installed by a serious mason costs more than stamped concrete, not less.

###### Our honest take

For a mountain cabin, a lakefront property, or a custom home where the patio is part of the architecture **stamped concrete wins most of the time** on both look and longevity. For a courtyard where you want to pull pieces up periodically (buried utilities, planters, design changes), pavers still have a role.
